2. A real-world problem and technology solution
A real-world problem I care about is food waste. Grocery stores, restaurants, and cafeterias throw away food that is still safe to eat, while many families struggle to afford enough healthy meals. I would build an app called FoodLink to connect businesses with extra food to food banks, shelters, community organizations, and families nearby.
Businesses could post photos of extra meals, produce, bread, or packaged food before it expires. Each post would include the pickup deadline, quantity, ingredients, allergens, storage requirements, and location. The app would first notify local food banks and shelters because they can distribute large amounts of food. If no organization can accept the donation, the app could offer pickup slots to verified community members. Volunteers could use the app to deliver food to people who do not have transportation.
FoodLink would use location services to find the closest available pickup option and reduce travel time. It could also predict when businesses are likely to have extra food based on past donations. For example, a bakery may have extra bread at the end of each day, while a restaurant may have extra prepared meals after an event. The app could send reminders before food expires and help businesses track how much food they donate.
I would work with food safety experts, local stores, and community organizations while building it. My goal would be to make donating food simple for businesses and make healthy food easier for families to access. This solution could reduce landfill waste, lower hunger, and strengthen connections between local organizations.
